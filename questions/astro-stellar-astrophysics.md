### what is a supernova
difficulty: basic
labels: supernovae, transients, stellar-evolution

What is a supernova, and what are its two main channels?

---

A supernova is a brief, extremely luminous stellar explosion. It can result from core collapse in a massive star or thermonuclear disruption of a carbon-oxygen white dwarf, usually a Type Ia event.

---

Core-collapse events leave neutron stars or black holes; Type Ia events leave no white-dwarf remnant and can be standardized as distance indicators.

- **Type Ia**: thermonuclear disruption of a carbon-oxygen white dwarf, no hydrogen or helium in the spectrum; standardizable candle.
- **Type II**: core collapse of a massive star that retained its hydrogen envelope; hydrogen lines present.
- **Type Ib/Ic**: core collapse of a massive star stripped of its hydrogen (Ib) or hydrogen and helium (Ic) envelope before explosion.

### luminosity
difficulty: basic
labels: luminosity, stellar-properties, photometry

What is luminosity in astrophysics, and how does it differ from observed flux?

---

Luminosity is the rate at which a source emits energy,

$$L = \frac{dE}{dt}.$$

It is an intrinsic power measured in watts, where 1 W = 1 J/s. For example, the Sun has $L_\odot\approx3.8\times10^{26}\,\mathrm{W}$.

---

The flux we observe at distance $d$ is

$$F = \frac{L}{4\pi d^2}.$$

Luminosity is the source's intrinsic energy output; flux or apparent brightness is the energy received per unit area. Bolometric luminosity covers all wavelengths, while band luminosity refers to a range such as $r$-band or X-ray, and luminosity may be expressed in solar units such as $10^5L_\odot$.
