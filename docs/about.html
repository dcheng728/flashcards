About

  • This project is built with the help of Claude Opus 4.5–5 and ChatGPT 5.6 Sol.
  • The goal is to explore human-AI interaction aimed at human understanding.

This project began in March 2026, during my MSc dissertation in theoretical physics at Imperial College London. I came to believe that the bottleneck in AI for physics is not model capability, but the researcher's ability to keep up with what the model explores. Work that no human can understand, verify, and answer for is not yet knowledge, however elegant or correct.

The best way to learn physics is to do problems. Yet my agent never checks my understanding as a good lecturer does, sensing drowsiness or puzzlement in the audience's eyes. It simply finishes the task and hands me a long markdown file.

So I built this site to let AI quiz me. The flashcards span many topics (classical, quantum, relativity, ...) and difficulty levels. Each card is self-graded from 1 to 3. The grades set a familiarity score that decays with a half-life of 133 hours. These ratings record what I know, so an AI agent with access to them knows it too. The intended workflow is that after a seminar or an agent-assisted research session, I can give the notes to a flashcard agent, which uses may past ratings to write cards just outside my comfort zone.

The more ambitious goal, not yet realized, is to put the flashcards and ratings in the research agent's context. The agent could then gauge what I know from my past performance, and stop to quiz me when its work goes beyond it.

Ultimately, I want AI to help me understand more, not just achieve more.
